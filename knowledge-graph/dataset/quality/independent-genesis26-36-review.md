# Independent source checklist: Genesis 26–36

Prepared 2026-10-29 from every verse of the supplied WEB Genesis 26–36. This is a source-derived editorial acceptance checklist, not a claim of exhaustive annotation or historical verification. It does not assume the current graph inventory.

## Core identity/family coverage expected for a major-character phase

- Laban parent of Leah and Rachel (29:16); Bethuel parent of Laban (28:5). The `son of Nahor` phrase in Jacob's 29:5 question must not overwrite the narrator's immediate parentage or collapse Nahor/Bethuel.
- Jacob spouses Leah (29:23–30), Rachel (29:28), Bilhah (30:4), and Zilpah (30:9). These are source descriptions, not endorsement or exclusive/lifetime marriage assertions.
- Jacob parent of the twelve named sons (35:22–26) and Dinah (34:1).
- Leah parent of Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun (35:23), and Dinah (34:1).
- Rachel parent of Joseph and Benjamin (35:24).
- Bilhah parent of Dan and Naphtali (35:25).
- Zilpah parent of Gad and Asher (35:26).
- Rachel/Leah siblings through narrator's two-daughter statement (29:16; explicit sister language 30:1). Bilhah servant of Rachel (29:29); Zilpah servant of Leah (29:24).
- Hamor parent of Shechem the person (34:2, 34:6), distinct from the city Shechem (33:18).

An omitted core relationship should be a named review warning, not silently filled from adjacency. Its absence need not invalidate every selective dataset; resolution must be explicit. Never create fictitious links merely to eliminate isolated nodes.

## Aliases explicitly supported

- Jacob / Israel: 32:28 and 35:10. Keep existing Jacob ID.
- Benoni / Benjamin: 35:18. The naming mother and father differ but referent is one child.
- Bethel / Luz: 28:19 and 35:6.
- Ephrath / Bethlehem: 35:19.
- Esau / Edom: person references in 36:1 and 36:8; do not attach all land/people uses of Edom to the individual.

## Required disambiguation checks

- `Israel` in 32:32 (children/community), 34:7 (collective social setting), 36:31 (children/community) is not an unqualified Jacob-person mention. `El Elohe Israel` in 33:20 names an altar and needs its own nested-name policy.
- Jacob's false claim `I am Esau` (27:19) is not an alias or same-as assertion. Literal quoted names can mention Esau as a claimed identity, but must not merge entities.
- Isaac's `She is my sister` (26:7) is a defensive claim; Rebekah remains his wife, not an established sister.
- Abimelech and Phicol in chapter 26 must not be silently merged with their Genesis 20–21 namesakes; identity continuity is an editorial uncertainty.
- Cain-line Adah (chapter 4) and Esau's Adah (36:2) are separate. Esau's wife names/fathers differ between 26:34, 28:9 and 36:2–3: do not force Adah/Basemath or Mahalath/Basemath equivalences. Preserve the local naming or omit with a warning.
- Shechem person vs city; Seir person (36:20) vs territory; Bela king (36:32) vs Bela/Zoar city (14:2); Timna person/concubine/chief; Anah parent/child names; Korah son/chief entries require explicit scope or unresolved handling.
- Distinct collective, genealogical, and territorial readings in chapter 36 must not be forced into individual persons solely because they are capitalized.

## Speech/events to preserve carefully

- Jacob's wrestler is an unnamed man (32:24). His later statement about seeing God (32:30) does not independently license a same-as God or angel edge.
- Esau plans/threatens to kill Jacob (27:41); no killing occurs. Reconciliation at 33:4 supports narrated embrace/meeting, not a permanent political relationship.
- Laban's allegations in 31:26–30 are speech. The narrator does say Rachel stole the teraphim (31:19, 31:32), and that Jacob did not know (31:32).
- The household livestock methods in chapter 30 are narrated, not an endorsed biological mechanism.
- Simeon and Levi jointly kill Hamor and Shechem (34:25–26). If materializing separate edges for each actor, mark the joint action rather than asserting independent killings.
- Dinah/Shechem: the coercive narrative is not a completed consensual marriage. Requests for her as wife (34:4, 34:8, 34:12) are not spouse evidence.
- Rachel dies and is buried on the way to Ephrath (35:19); do not strengthen this to death inside Bethlehem.
- Esau and Jacob bury Isaac (35:29); exact burial location requires other evidence, not assumed from preceding residence.

## Useful but not exhaustive narrative/place connections

- Isaac resides in Gerar (26:6), travels to Beersheba (26:23), and exchanges oaths with Abimelech's party (26:26–31).
- Jacob travels to Paddan Aram/Laban (28:5), to Succoth (33:17), to Shechem (33:18), and to Bethel/Luz (35:6); Esau returns to Seir (33:16).
- Jacob/Laban covenant is spoken/proposed (31:44) and accompanied by a sworn act (31:53); evidence should include the latter if modeled as completed.
- Deborah is Rebekah's nurse and dies/buried below Bethel (35:8); do not merge with later Bible namesakes.
